I've spent a significant amount of my life at this point looking at a computer screen, monitor, smartphone, etc. Yet, I really don't think I have any idea how to use this technology well. I thought for a while that I would be able to journal on a notebook instead, but I realized that I actually don't know how to use that technology well either. Honestly, I'm not quite sure why in either case. 

I've been to school. I've watched youtube videos. I've seen others do it. Even setting up this blog, in 2026 (the time of AI) presented surprising challenges. I figured I would just do a little by hand, maybe that was silly. I'm not anti-AI. There are technologies that I don't care for, and I do recognize that those have made a significant impact on the world, even positively. I am an ex-motorist, and I would likely rally around an anti-car flag should one be raised. 

Peers of mine gathered today to discuss considerations for the world and work given the current state of AI. The prompts and answers were loaded, but the hour of discussion did give me real time to shape clearer thoughts on the topic. I am concerned with the loss of source material pre-AI, for example. In the extreme case, I've read (thanks to the incredible reporting by folks at 404 Media, like Emanuel Maiberg) that Amazon and others are buying and destroying books to train their models. [1] That is pretty terrifying. Then I thought more about all of the issues that were in my head and at its core, the bigger issue is who has money to fund these sorts of things and is willing to fund it in pursuit of further wealth, self-benefit, etc., which really boils down to some of the fundamental issues with the shape of capitalism in America, and the perverse incentive structures for wealth accrual in our markets. AI, like nuclear power, is an extremely useful technology, but we just need to be willing to invest more significant resources figuring out how to use it well and safely before rushing into a state of chaos and disaster, as these technologies effect everyone in the world. Regarding safety, I'm not talking about the scary tactics companies are using to sell AI services (this is a problem that has been the case since social media, and likely with many technologies/inventions before it: plastic, coke, cigarettes, etc.), but I'm talking about allowing the frontier labs to just do whatever they want with it and get away with it. Just as we finally realized with things like social media and advertising that we need to fine companies for doing abusive things with personal private data, we need to wake up and realize that AI companies can't just let their technology commit fraud and abuse across computer systems owned by other entities. We can't let them destroy data or pollute data for competitive advantage. We can't let them waste land, silicon and energy without making them put in real work. We are essentially letting them use the world as their playground, which is exactly what they are doing right now. As far as I can tell, this latest generation of AI started from playing around and it really hasn't stopped being essentially that. I definitely got yelled at as a kid and had the cops called on me when I saw the world as one big skatepark and would skate on loading docks or across gas stations. The stakes seem much higher than just a skid mark on some concrete. These are publicity stunts for these companies. It seems to be a competition around which company was least responsible and most childish with how they ran their AI. Maybe fine them for as much as people have invested in them. Whichever generation of model was the leader fine them the cost of training it. I just don't understand why everyone is so willing to be made a fool in this situation. 

All this talk of singularity is just distraction. AI is very useful today, and will continue to be useful even if we stopped at the latest generation of model. I followed this author, Roots of Progress, and I attended a couple of ... meetings, and one of the things that stood out to me was that they kept talking about deregulation, like government was the sole problem for why we weren't making meaningful progress. This was pre-trump 2 so maybe they were right, but at the time, I wasn't sure why they weren't more concerned with companies that lobby to maintain their monopoly status over an industry or market. As far as I understand it, lobbyist are the reason we have much of the random regulation we have today that leads to things like making it more challenging for people to grow and sell their own food for instance. The government has a hand in this, but they are just a conduit for our large and greedy corporations to keep other people from innovating and diversifying in their space. Frontier labs are using aggressive tactics to try to do similar things today so I'm cautious around what actual the government should actually take. I don't think we should stop AI companies from existing and doing meaningful work, but I do think we should stop them from abusing resources, both private and public, in the name of play or whatever the hell it is that they are doing. I don't know if it should be substantial financial punishment or forcing them to find different energy sources, like clean and durable nuclear, or forcing them to think about their architectures more deeply before we waste everyone's compute and silicon building out infrastructure that might not even be necessary in a year from now, or force them to open source them weights or architectures so that everyone can innovate in the space and their work was all built on the work of everyone else's in the world. If we are really scared of anything, it is letting these folks continue to just freely play with the most expensive technologies the world has ever made (and be the only ones able to do so) while we all wait to see the next dumb thing they let it do. Social media be damned, but (and even those this is hard), you really don't have to use it. I don't. It will never be that way with AI from this point forward and we should be able to shape the usage and integration points.

I was just thinking about Microsoft and how they wanted to get a PC in every person's home. There was bell labs. I don't know. If I ran an experiment with an AI agent and it randomly hacked some federal government website because it thought the answer to 2+2=X was in their database somewhere, I would go to prison. People should invest in companies that are making the current AI architectures more sustainable in every way, not the companies that have no clue what to do with the innovations they made other than let it hack someone else. 

This ended up being a bit more stream of consciousness than I would have hoped. I also am not anti frontier lab, but I just don't think they can be so reckless without real consequence. I'm sure I will have some posts where I just ask (random) model to do something that even a year ago I wouldn't have thought possible, which is pretty incredible, but still.

Turns out, I'm not a fan of this theme, and I'm editing this in vim with config settings that are pretty miserable for editing. Maybe I will stop watching youtube videos and just use a computer for once. Heh, there was a time in college when I was doing my programming languages class homework on my phone ssh'd to a vps box. Even the tech committee for DSA uses google docs though.
